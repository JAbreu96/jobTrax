"Interviewed" means a real evaluation happened — technical, behavioral, system design, take-home, pairing or a final round. Recruiter and phone screens stop at "at screen": they filter for plausibility rather than testing whether you can do the job, so counting them here would inflate the stage with calls that reject almost nobody. "At screen" counts anyone sitting at a screening status now or with a screening round logged — status holds current state only, so a job screened and then rejected would otherwise forget it ever got there. It remains a floor, not a total, until the interview log fills in.

What the columns mean. Led to another round — something came after it, or the job ended in an offer; this round did not stop you. Ended in rejection — it was the last round and the job is now rejected, so the process ended here. Went silent — last round, the job never closed, and nothing for {{ ghost_days }}+ days. Nobody said no; they stopped replying. It counts against the rate because it demonstrably did not advance you, but it is shown separately so you can see how much of a low rate is silence rather than rejection — which is usually about the company, not about you. No result yet — the most recent round, still inside the {{ ghost_days }}-day window. Excluded from the rate entirely: counting it as a failure would punish you for a process that simply has not finished.
Advance rate is how many rounds led to another one, out of those that reached a conclusion. It stays as plain counts until {{ stats.rate_min_rounds }} rounds have concluded — a percentage over two or three is a claim the sample cannot support, and "0%" would read as a verdict rather than a small number of calls.

Identity is the sender address for email, and the sender's display name for LinkedIn — InMail arrives from a shared relay (inmail-hit-reply@linkedin.com), so the address identifies LinkedIn, not the person, and the body carries no profile link to fall back on. One role can appear under two recruiters when two agencies pitch it, so the Roles column can total more than the captured count above; that is two relationships on one role, not a miscount. Recruiters added here are keyed on their email address, the same as one found in the inbox, so a later message from them lands on the row you typed instead of making a second one — and inbox-triage will not overwrite a name or agency you entered by hand. A suspected row is still best closed out by re-running triage against the original message, which is the only place the recruiter's identity can actually be read.
